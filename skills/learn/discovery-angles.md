# Discovery Angles

These six angles are prompts to help you spot candidate **Beliefs** during `Learn` Steps 4–5. They are thinking aids, not required folders or mandatory fields—though a Belief can carry one as an optional `tag`.

Remember: facts about what a metric means or how it is calculated are **not** Beliefs; they belong in Business Understanding (SPEC §4.4). Every candidate Belief you spot here must still pass the **Master Test** (SPEC §4.5):
1. It says something no single document states on its own.
2. It connects two or more Business Understanding entries.
3. It implies a concrete action or reading rule for a named deliverable.

---

## 1. How the business makes money (Business model)
- **Ask:** How does this business actually earn and keep money, and which engine is doing the heavy lifting right now?
- **Look for:** Shifts in where growth comes from (volume vs. price vs. mix), which product lines carry profit margins, and which costs are structural vs. discretionary.
- **Test:** Would this statement describe the same underlying reality regardless of which quarter you read? If it only describes one quarter, it is a result, not a belief.

## 2. How the numbers move together (Business dynamics)
- **Ask:** What leads, what lags, and where does a chain break?
- **Look for:** Lead-lag relationships, conversion funnels, offsetting lines, feedback loops, and recurring seasonality. Confirmed relationships also become Dependency Map links (SPEC §4.4).
- **Test:** Would this still hold even if the entire management team changed? It describes how the business mechanics behave, not how people decide.

## 3. How results are explained (Causal attribution)
- **Ask:** What does the team blame or credit for its results, and how reliable are those explanations?
- **Look for:** Recurring excuses for misses, which causes are named first, which are consistently ignored, and attributions that later get quietly revised.
- **Test:** Could a reader predict what the team will say the next time they miss, and know whether to take that explanation at face value or dig deeper?

## 4. How the organization behaves under pressure (Organizational behavior)
- **Ask:** What does this company actually do when things get tight, compared to what it promises?
- **Look for:** Which budgets get protected vs. cut, how fast leadership reacts, and which commitments survive a bad quarter.
- **Test:** Could a reader predict the company's next move in a similar situation—and recognize a break from that pattern as a real signal?

## 5. Story vs. numbers (Narrative versus numbers)
- **Ask:** How is the story told, and where does the narrative drift away from what the numbers show?
- **Look for:** Recurring report structures, what gets put at the top vs. buried in footnotes, and subtle wording shifts that show up before the numbers turn.
- **Test:** Could a reader predict the layout of the next report, and spot immediately when a change in tone signals trouble?

## 6. How forecasts and plans behave (Forecast and plan behavior)
- **Ask:** How are budgets and forecasts set and revised over time?
- **Look for:** Systematic bias in revisions (always too optimistic early in the year?), timing of resets, and which assumptions are chronically conservative or aggressive. Express patterns as directions and relative sizes—never raw numbers (SPEC Principle 2).
- **Test:** Could a reader predict the direction and rough relative size of the next forecast revision from the pattern alone?

---

## How to use these angles
- Apply them **after** the blind read and comparison—never before (SPEC Principle 5).
- It is completely normal for an angle to turn up nothing in a given pass. Never force a candidate.
- If two angles point to the same insight, propose one Belief and include both tags.
- Follow the plain-sentence wording rules in SPEC §4.5.
